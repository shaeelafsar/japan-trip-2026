1. Read this first

  • Early starts are flagged in the plan. Fri Nov 27 (Fuji) is the only day that needs a truly early start (out by 06:45). Mon Nov 30 and Tue Dec 1 start at 09:00. Every other day starts at 10:00 to 11:00, which is your normal rhythm.
  • Daylight is short. Sunset is about 16:30 every day. Plan sights and wooded walks between about 09:30 and 15:30, and use the dark hours for lights, observatories, indoor shows and dinner.
  • Every day has a break (a long seated lunch, a museum, a bus ride or a hotel rest). The two long days are Sat Nov 28 and Fri Nov 27, and both have a cut list.
  • Late nights stay in well-lit, busy places with an easy train home. See section 11.
  • Hikes avoid bear-alert areas. Mt Takao (alert level 1 and a bear filmed 70 m off Trail 1), Mitake and Oyama are left out. The two walks that do count as hikes are the Kamakura Daibutsu forest course and the Chureito stair climb. Both are heavily used, and the reports found no bear alert for Kamakura. Yamanashi has only a general bear mention (snippet-only), so stay on the maintained paths at Chureito.
  • Nights away are undecided, so Option A (no nights away) is the main plan. Section 7 swaps in one night at Kawaguchiko or Hakone. Section 8 swaps in two nights in Kyoto and Nara.
  • Assumption to confirm: I guessed you may be jet-lagged and awake early on days 1 to 3. The plan does not depend on it.

  • Temperatures (Japan Guide, from Japan Meteorological Agency data): November averages about 17 °C high and 10 °C low; December about 12 °C high and 5 °C low. For our dates expect roughly 12 to 16 °C by day and 5 to 9 °C at night (my interpolation). It will feel colder on rooftops, on the Kamakura coast and at Kawaguchiko (a rule of thumb is 5 to 8 °C colder than Tokyo, my estimate).
  • Rain and sun: late November has about 20 to 25 percent rainy days and 55 to 60 percent sunny days; early December is drier (10 to 15 percent rainy, 65 to 75 percent sunny). Japan Guide's table gives two values per month, which I read as early and late month. Winter air usually gives clear Fuji views but not on every day.
  • Pack: layers with a windproof outer layer, a compact umbrella, shoes with grip for the Kamakura trail, gloves and a hat for the observatories and Fuji.
  • Rush hour peaks 08:00 to 09:00 and after 17:00 (Japan Guide). Your 10:00 to 11:00 starts miss the morning peak; the 06:45 Fuji start and the 09:00 starts do not, so travel light on those days.

3. Where to stay

Shinjuku, on the south or west side (near the South, New South or West exits), not Kabukicho or Golden Gai. Why:

  • Day trips leave from here. Kamakura (about 55 to 65 minutes, Shonan-Shinjuku Line), Kawaguchiko (highway bus from Busta Shinjuku, about 1 hour 45 minutes, ¥2,200 each way) and Hakone (Odakyu Romancecar) all start in Shinjuku.
  • The plan's food is here. Tsunahachi Annex, Imahan, Isetan with Toraya and Sushi Zanmai are all in or next to Shinjuku.
  • It has easy hotel breaks because Shibuya, Harajuku and Ginza are 7 to 16 minutes away by train.
  • Haneda is one airport bus or one train change away (about 60 to 75 minutes, my estimate).
  • Alternative: Tokyo Station if you choose the Kyoto option (Option C), because the Shinkansen leaves from there.

6. Weather and swap rules

  • Fuji day (Fri Nov 27) is the flex day. About a week out, check the forecast for Fri and Mon. If Fri looks cloudy or wet and Mon looks clear, swap Fuji and Kamakura (Kamakura Fri, Fuji Mon). Fuji needs a clear forecast; Kamakura tolerates overcast. Book the bus with an eye to its change policy, and hold refundable dinner bookings.
  • Both days wet: do Kamakura's temples without the trail, and swap Fuji for indoor items (Tokyo National Museum, teamLab, the sushi class, the Samurai Ninja Museum, the Isetan food hall).
  • Closures that fix the order: Shinjuku Gyoen and the Tokyo National Museum are closed Mon Nov 30. Tokyo National Museum's Toyokan and Honkan Rooms 17 to 20 close from Nov 30. The Imperial East Gardens are closed Mon and Fri. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government south observatory is closed Tue Dec 1.
  • Colour is a late-window bet. Tokyo's foliage usually peaks late November to early December, and Kawaguchiko's is mostly over. Do not build a day around it.

7. Option B: one night away

B1. Kawaguchiko overnight, Fri Nov 27 to Sat Nov 28 (replaces the Fuji day trip and eases the 06:45 start)

  • Fri: bus about 08:30 (arrive about 10:15), Chureito, lunch, Oishi Park, check in somewhere with a Fuji view and an onsen. Take a small overnight bag and leave the suitcase at the Shinjuku hotel.
  • Sat: Fuji at sunrise (06:30), breakfast, bus back about 11:00 (arrive about 13:00 to 14:00).
  • Sat afternoon: go straight to the Tokyo National Museum (open to 20:00), then T's Tantan and teamLab as in the main plan. Panga lunch and Ueno Park drop out.
  • Gain: better Fuji light and a slower Friday. Cost: lodging near the Momiji Festival (to Nov 29) is booked up early, and a Saturday highway bus is busy. Ask the lodging in advance for a pork-free dinner.

B2. Hakone overnight, Mon Nov 30 to Tue Dec 1 (replaces Kamakura and Tuesday's Asakusa morning)

  • Mon: Odakyu Romancecar from Shinjuku (Freepass ¥7,100, ¥9,500 with Romancecar), Old Tokaido cedar avenue and the Moto-Hakone walk (3 to 5 hours), the Lake Ashi cruise as a transit leg only, onsen at the lodging.
  • Tue: back by about 13:00, then Senso-ji and the sushi class in the afternoon, and the Ginza and Marunouchi evening as in the main plan.
  • The ropeway can stop for wind or gas on any day; its Dec 7 to 24 suspension is after our dates.

8. Option C: Kyoto and Nara

Keeps Thu, Fri and Sat in Tokyo unchanged. Replaces Shinjuku Gyoen, the sumo show, Kamakura and Asakusa. Cost: about ¥28,000 to 31,000 per adult return by Shinkansen (2 hours 15 minutes each way), plus two Kyoto nights.

DayPlan
Sun Nov 29Shinkansen to Kyoto in the morning. Fushimi Inari in the afternoon (free, open 24 hours). Kiyomizu-dera night viewing (Nov 21 to 30, to 21:30).
Mon Nov 30Nara: Todai-ji (¥800, hall 08:00 to 17:00) and the park. Back in Kyoto by early evening. Eikando's illumination (Nov 20 to Dec 6, snippet-only) is the evening option. Nijo Castle's Ninomaru is closed Tue Dec 1, so skip it or fit it into Sunday.
Tue Dec 1Kyoto morning, Shinkansen back to Tokyo by midday, then the Ginza eel dinner and Marunouchi lights as in the main plan.
Wed Dec 2Senso-ji at 07:00 before the flight is possible if you check out early (check whether a direct Asakusa to Haneda train fits with your bags).

Kyoto's foliage likely peaks late November to early December (my knowledge, to verify), which makes this option the best match for the season. It costs about three Tokyo days of content.

10. Left out, and why

  • Mt Takao, Mitake and Oyama: bear alerts or incident clusters, and Takao Trail 6 is uphill-only. Takao is an opt-in swap only if you accept a busy trail with the alert-level-1 notice.
  • Rikugien night illumination: a good B-rated item, but ticket sales were not announced and the daily cap is a risk. It is the Sunday alternative.
  • Tokyo Metropolitan Government observatory: free and good, but it duplicates SHIBUYA SKY. It is the fallback if SKY sells out.
  • Imperial East Gardens, Hamarikyu, Zojo-ji and Tokyo Tower, Akihabara shopping, Yanaka, Kichijoji and Inokashira Park: fine, but not needed once the days are full. They are the swap pool for a rain day or a cancelled booking.
  • Samurai Ninja Museum, kimono rental, tea or matcha class, teamLab Planets: swap candidates. Pick only one teamLab.
  • Skytree deck, Nikko, Jigokudani snow monkeys, Ghibli Museum (closed Dec 1), Yomiuriland, street go-karts: weak fit for these dates or this group, per the reports.
  • Food not scheduled: Isehiro yakitori-don, Kanda Yabusoba (closed Wednesdays), Maruka udon, Ginza Kagari ramen, Hiiragi (only if passing). Add them as lunch swaps.
  • Ameyoko: crowded and mostly pork street food.
