More

Daylight, weather and packing
  • Sunrise ~06:30, sunset ~16:30, dark by about 17:00 (Tokyo, Kamakura and Takao within 2 minutes of each other). Plan trails and outdoor stops to finish before 15:30.
  • Tokyo averages 15 °C high and 7 °C low in late November, 13 and 5 °C in early December (Japan Meteorological Agency 1991 to 2020 normals, not a forecast). Cold snaps happen.
  • Rain: about 1 day in 4 in late November and about 1 in 6 in early December (days with 1 mm or more, same normals).
  • Day trips are colder. Mt Takao's summit is about 3 °C below Tokyo. Fuji's lakes run about 3 °C colder by day and 6 to 7 °C colder at night, near freezing. Kamakura is about the same as Tokyo.
  • Autumn colour (Japan Meteorological Corporation forecast, Oct 1; it updates in early November): Tokyo ginkgo about Nov 26 and maple about Nov 28. Takao peaks about Nov 22 and fades through Nov 29. Rikugien peaks about Dec 2 and Kamakura about Dec 3. Fuji's lake colour is likely over.
  • Fuji views are a bet. Cold mornings improve the odds but cloud often hides it, and snow or ice can close the 5th Station road. I found no honest percentage.
  • Pack: layers, a windproof and waterproof shell, gloves, a hat, a compact umbrella and grippy shoes for the trails.
  • Rush hour peaks 08:00 to 09:00 and after 17:00. The Sun, Mon and Tue early starts hit it, so travel light those days.
Where to stay (not booked)

Shinjuku, south or west side (South, New South or West exits), not Kabukicho or Golden Gai.

  • Kamakura (JR direct), Takao (Keio Line from Shinjuku) and the Fuji tour pickups all leave from here.
  • Tsunahachi, Imahan, Isetan, Shinjuku Gyoen and Sushi Zanmai are nearby.
  • Shibuya, Harajuku and Ginza are 7 to 16 minutes by train. Haneda is about 60 to 75 minutes.
  • For Option C, stay near Tokyo Station instead.
Weather and swap rules
  • Fuji is the flex day. Check the forecast about a week out. A guided tour is easier to move or cancel only if its terms allow it, so read them before you book.
  • Closed days fix the order. Shinjuku Gyoen and the Tokyo National Museum close Mon Nov 30, so neither is planned for Monday. Shinjuku's south observatory closes Tue Dec 1.
  • Pacing: Fri to Tue are five full days in a row (early Fri and Sun, long Sat and Tue). Each has a break, but this is the tiring stretch. If energy drops, cut in this order: Sat Ueno Park stroll, Mon Yuigahama, Tue Marunouchi lights, then the Mon trail (use its temples-only backup).
  • Sun and Mon are both walking days. If legs are tired after Takao, take the Mon backup (temples only, no trail).
  • Both days wet: swap Takao for Shinjuku Gyoen or Ueno, keep the indoor items (museum, teamLab, classes, Isetan) and do Kamakura's temples in rain gear.
  • Autumn colour is a bonus, not a reason to move a day. Forecasts shift, so recheck in early November.
  • Kintsugi was cut for karting on Tue Dec 1. Book it instead if karting falls through: Kintsugi workshops.
Tea farms: what I found

Late November is off-season for picking. Operators put picking in May to October. No page says the plants are dormant now, so expect farm visits and tastings, not picking.

  • Sanopy half-day (the swap on Thu 26): GetYourGuide or Viator. 4 hours, about $76 each, Nov 26 and 29 only.
  • Shizuoka private tasting: Viator. 7 hours, from $271 per person, every date Nov 25 to Dec 2. Meet 9:00 at Shizuoka Station (the train is extra). Moriuchi family farm plus Nihondaira views. 10 reviews, 100 percent recommend. Pick a pork-free lunch.
  • Private car tour to the Fuji tea fields: GetYourGuide. 10 hours, about $416 per group of up to 6, hotel pickup around 07:00. Fields are seen from the road only. Dec 1 and 2 were not confirmed. New listing, no ratings.
  • Standard Fuji bus tours have no tea-field stop. The ones I opened offer only a 10 to 45 minute matcha session at a shop.
  • Blocked sources: Viator, GetYourGuide and Klook block automated reading, so I read these pages in a browser on Oct 20. Prices and slots change, so recheck before booking.
Option B: a night in Hakone (Mon 30 to Tue 1)

Replaces the Kamakura trail. Hakone is a sightseeing loop: ropeway, lake cruise, museum and onsen. It is colder than Tokyo by 4 to 7 °C.

  • Mon: Odakyu Romancecar from Shinjuku (Freepass ¥7,100, or ¥9,500 with Romancecar). Ropeway, Lake Ashi cruise, Open-Air Museum, onsen at the inn.
  • The old Tokaido cedar avenue in Hakone is a busy walk worth adding if the weather is dry. I did not verify its current path conditions.
  • Tue: back by about 13:00, then Senso-ji and the Bincho dinner. The sushi class, Samurai museum and karting drop out.
  • The ropeway can stop for wind or gas. Ask the inn for a pork-free dinner.
Option C: two nights in Kyoto and Nara (Sun 29 to Tue 1)

Keeps Thu to Sat as planned. Costs about ¥28,000 to 31,000 per adult by Shinkansen (2 h 15 min each way), plus two nights.

  • Sun: Fushimi Inari in the afternoon, then Kiyomizu-dera night viewing (to Nov 30). The summit loop is a popular walk but needs daylight (sunset about 16:30), so start it before 14:00.
  • Mon: Nara for Todai-ji and the park, back to Kyoto for the evening.
  • Tue: Kyoto morning, train back, then Ginza eel and the Marunouchi lights.
  • Best match for autumn colour. Costs about three Tokyo days: Mt Takao, sumo, the Kamakura trail, the Asakusa day and karting.
Evenings: safety and getting home
  • Every night ends in a busy, well-lit area with a direct train home.
  • Sun (sumo and sushi) is at Okubo, the edge of Kabukicho. Go straight to the venue and decline bar invitations.
  • Skip Shibuya's nightlife streets and Roppongi street level. The UK Foreign Office warns about touts and drink spiking there.
Ordering card: no pork, no alcohol

Show this at restaurants. Written by me, not checked by a Japanese speaker: have one confirm it before you print it.

  • 豚肉は食べられません。 (I cannot eat pork.)
  • 豚エキス、ラード、豚骨スープもだめです。 (No pork extract, lard or pork-bone soup.)
  • お酒は飲みません。料理にも酒やみりんを使わないでください。 (I do not drink alcohol. Please do not use sake or mirin in the cooking.)
  • No restaurant is certified pork-free. Broths and fryer oil are the usual hidden sources.
Left out, and why
  • Mt Fuji summit and the 5th Station: only with a guide. That is your call, and it is the one place I treated as unsafe on its own.
  • Mt Mitake and Mt Oyama: not planned. They are bear habitat and I could not confirm continuous weekday traffic. I can add either if you want one.
  • Mt Nokogiri: a busy trail I found but did not plan. Ask and I will fit it in.
  • Kintsugi: moved to the swap pool when karting took its Tue slot, as you chose.
  • Skytree deck, Nikko, snow monkeys, Sanrio Puroland, Fuji-Q: weak fit for these dates.
  • Ghibli Museum: closed Dec 1.
  • Swap pool: teamLab Planets, Rikugien illumination (Nov 25 to Dec 6, ¥1,000 to 1,200), Hamarikyu, Sumida riverside, Imperial Palace East Gardens, the Meiji Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue (peak about Nov 27), Isehiro and Kanda Yabusoba lunches, kintsugi.
