Quick take

  • Six A-tier walks and hikes: Meiji Jingu and Harajuku, Asakusa and the Sumida, Mt Takao, Ueno and Yanaka, the Kamakura Daibutsu forest trail and Shinjuku Gyoen. Takao and Kamakura are the real hikes; the rest are flat city walks.
  • Bear warning: a bear was filmed about 70 m off Mt Takao Trail 1 in August (local alert level 1), and the Mitake and Okutama area has the worst bear data. Hike in daylight, in a group, make noise and check local notices. Mt Mitake is demoted to C.
  • Takao Trail 6 is uphill-only from Oct 31 to Dec 6, 2026 (official notice). Take Trail 1 or the cable car down.
  • Start wooded walks early and finish by about 15:30. Sunset is about 16:30. Shinjuku Gyoen, the Imperial East Gardens and the Takao visitor centre are closed Mon Nov 30.
  • Redone with Google Maps, AllTrails, TripAdvisor and official pages. Reddit stayed blocked, so no thread was read and no pick is rated high confidence. These are alternatives, not a checklist: pick one or two walks per day.

Meiji Jingu, Yoyogi Park and Harajuku

A · Strongest fit

The best-rated free walk in Tokyo: the forest approach to Meiji Jingu, Yoyogi Park and then Harajuku's shopping streets in one connected route. The shrine gate closes before 16:30, so go in the morning, and expect Takeshita Street to be a crush.

Route: Harajuku Station, the forest approach to the main shrine, Yoyogi Park, then Takeshita Street and Omotesando as an add-on (iteration 1) [S49]. Takeshita Street at its busiest is a shuffle; the parallel streets are calmer (iteration 1) [S49].

Cost per adultFree
Time2.5-4 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaShibuya, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: The shrine gate closes at 16:10 in November and Takeshita Street is a crush.

Why it ranks here: The best-rated free walk in Tokyo: a forest approach, a large park and a shopping district in one connected route; the shrine gate closes before 16:30, so go in the morning.

Asakusa, Sumida riverside and Skytree approach

A · Strongest fit
Sonnet Memory Overlap

Tokyo's most-visited temple, Senso-ji, then a flat riverside stroll along the Sumida to the Skytree. The temple is the draw; the Nakamise shopping street is the weakest-rated part. Crowded after about 09:00.

Route: Kaminarimon, Nakamise and the main hall; the back streets; the Sumida riverside park; then the Skytree approach (iteration 1) [S49]. The Skytree deck is optional and duplicates the free observatory views in the Must-see report [S51].

Cost per adultFree
Time2.5-4 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaTaito and Sumida, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Temple crowds after about 09:00; the riverside is breezy.

Why it ranks here: Tokyo's most-reviewed temple plus a flat riverside walk to the Skytree; the temple is the draw, and the shopping street is the weakest-rated part.

Memory overlap: Kimono rental and stroll in Asakusa (same area; now researched in iteration 2). The rank was reviewed against it and left as is.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps Senso-ji 4.6 (100,309), Nakamise 4.3 (15,870), Sumida Park 4.2 (4,614) and Tokyo Skytree 4.4 (120,485) [S41]; marketplace Asakusa walking tours Viator 5.0 (1,228) and 4.9 (1,125), GetYourGuide 4.8 (1,993), Klook about 4.9 (304) [S44, S45, S46] (Viator and TripAdvisor tour counts are one pool). Approximate cost

Crowds and booking

Late-November / early-December fit

Mt Takao: Trail 1, cable car and Yakuo-in

A · Strongest fit
Sonnet Memory Overlap

The best-rated hill walk within an hour of Shinjuku, in its busiest foliage fortnight: ride the cable car or walk Trail 1 to Yakuo-in temple and the summit (599 m). Go early on a weekday. A bear was filmed near Trail 1 in August and Trail 6 is uphill-only, so read the notices first.

Route: Takaosanguchi Station, the Kiyotaki cable car or chair lift (or walk Trail 1), Yakuo-in, then the summit; return by the same route. Do not descend Trail 6: it is slippery, busy and has no toilets or water [S36]. Optional: the monkey park, the onsen and the free Takao 599 Museum [S26].

Cost per adultTrail free; cable car ¥490 each way
Time3.5-5 hours on the mountain
ConfidenceMedium
AreaHachioji, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Bear alert near Trail 1, Trail 6 uphill-only, cable-car queues and early dusk.

Why it ranks here: The best-rated hill walk within an hour of Shinjuku, in its busiest foliage fortnight; do it early on a weekday, stay on Trail 1, take the cable car down or up, and heed the bear and Trail 6 notices.

Memory overlap: Mount Mitake (now researched in iteration 2, demoted to C for bears; another hike). The rank was reviewed against it and left as is.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps Mount Takao 4.5 (5,275), Takaosan Yakuo-in 4.4 (7,575), the Omiharashidai viewpoint 4.5 (1,193), the Kiyotaki cable car 4.1 (2,677), Takao Trail 6 and Biwa Falls 4.4 (148) and Takao Trail 1 4.3 (25) [S42]; TripAdvisor Mt Takao 4.3 (1,232), #1 of 145 in Hachioji [S40]; Japan Guide 4.0 (454) [S26]; AllTrails Mount Takao Inariyama Trail (Trail 1) 4.6 (504) [S39]; GetYourGuide's only Takao product is a 5.0 (114) tour with ramen and a hot spring (see section 5) [S45]. Single-author blog (July 2026) says Trail 1 gains the same roughly 400 m as the others but early [S36].
Location and access
Keio Line from Shinjuku to Takaosanguchi, 430 yen, about 50 minutes; the JR Chuo Line to Takao (720 yen) plus Keio (140 yen) is the alternative [S26].
Time and physical effort
Official Trail 1 is 3.8 km, fully concrete-paved, with a standard time of 1 hour 40 minutes up and 1 hour 20 minutes down, and many steps above Yakuo-in; the slope below the lift is steep when wet [S03]. AllTrails lists the whole Trail 1 route as a 4.9 mi (7.9 km) loop with 1,309 ft (399 m) of ascent and 3-3.5 hours [S39]. Japan Guide says Trail 1 takes about 90 minutes from the base, halved by the cable car or lift [S26]. Cable car up and down is about 2.5-3 hours (iteration 1) [S49].
Approximate cost
Trail free; cable car or lift 490 yen each way (980 return) [S26, S49]; Keio about 860 return.
Crowds and booking
No reservation. Japan Guide says the second half of November is the busiest period [S26]; one snippet-only source (thestandardjapan.com, 2026-09-28) says the cable car wait can reach 60-90 minutes between 09:00 and noon in autumn peaks [S47]. The visitor centre is open 10:00-16:00 and closed Mondays [S02].
Late-November / early-December fit
Good: the Takao foliage and the busy-season timing both fall in the window. The cable car runs 08:00-17:45 in Sep-Nov and to 17:15 in December; the chair lift runs 09:00-16:30 (16:00 from December) [S26]. Trail 6 is uphill-only all day from Oct 31 to Dec 6, 2026, between Biwa Falls and the Trail 5 junction, to ease congestion and prevent accidents; an old "until Dec 1, 08:00-14:00" notice on Yamakei is a stale 2024 page [S01, S25]. Not feasible on arrival evening; Wed 2 Dec is too tight.
Safety and constraints
Bear: a wildlife camera recorded an adult bear about 70 m off Trail 1, near Shiromidai, on Aug 20; the city classed it "alert level 1" and asked people to avoid the dark hours [S18]; officials advise a group, a bell or radio, and avoiding early morning, evening and night [S19]. KumaWatch rates the area "alert" with 15 sightings within 10 km in 90 days, latest Oct 19, though not necessarily inside the mountain's grounds [S20]; Kumamap logs 18 incidents in 30 days [S21]. Other notices: a fallen-tree caution on the Konpira-dai course and a landslide-affected side path near Kobotoke [S02]. Finish by about 15:30.
Uncertainty / what to verify
The latest bear notice and any closure on the day; whether the cable car and Trail 1 lower section are crowded; Mitake-style bear data (item 17) does not apply to Takao but the regional risk does; Reddit not opened.

Ueno Park, Ameyoko, Yanaka Ginza and Nezu Shrine

A · Strongest fit

A long, flat walk through Ueno Park, the Ameyoko market street, Nezu Shrine, Yanaka cemetery and the old-town Yanaka Ginza shopping street. The street-snack stops need pork screening.

Route: Ueno Park, Ameyoko, Nezu Shrine, Yanaka Cemetery and Yanaka Ginza, in either direction (iteration 1) [S49]. Yanaka Ginza is 5-10 minutes from Nippori Station and the cemetery is 3 minutes from it [S31]. Yanaka Ginza is sleepy early and busy in the afternoon (iteration 1) [S49].

Cost per adultFree
Time3-4.5 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaTaito and Bunkyo, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Snack streets may serve pork; the route is long and flat.

Why it ranks here: A long, flat and varied walk through a park, a market street and an old-town shopping street; the guided-tour ratings are strong, but the snack stops need pork screening.

More details and sources

The ratings for the shopping streets are lower than for the park and shrine.

Kamakura: Daibutsu hiking course, Great Buddha and Hase-dera

A · Strongest fit

A short forest trail (the Daibutsu course, 3.6 km) from Kita-Kamakura over the hills to the Great Buddha, then Hase-dera temple and the coast. The easiest real trail here, but muddy, with no water, and the temples close at 17:00.

Route: Kita-Kamakura Station, then up mossy steps by Jochi-ji to Kuzuharaoka Shrine, Genjiyama Park and down to Daibutsu-zaka; then the Great Buddha (Kotoku-in) and Hase-dera [S37]. The optional Zeniarai Benten detour adds about 31 minutes [S37]. Japan Guide says the Ten-en course is best for autumn colour in early December and the Daibutsu course takes 60-90 minutes [S28]. Tsurugaoka Hachimangu is an optional extra.

Cost per adult¥700 for two temples
Time4-6 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaKamakura, Kanagawa

Main tradeoff: A muddy 3.6 km forest trail with no water; temples close at 17:00.

Why it ranks here: A short forest trail that ends at the Great Buddha, with a temple and a coast to follow; the easiest "real" trail on this list.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps Kotoku-in 4.4 (33,169), Hase-dera 4.5 (17,371), Tsurugaoka Hachimangu 4.4 (29,577), the Daibutsu trail entrance 4.5 (30) and Komachi-dori 4.5 (43) [S43]; AllTrails Kuzuharaoka-Daibutsu Hiking Course 4.6 (358) [S39]; TripAdvisor Daibutsukiritoshi 4.2 (38) [S40]; Japan Guide Kamakura Hiking Trails 4.0 (373) [S28]; marketplace Viator Kamakura Historical Walk with Great Buddha 4.9 (176) and Kamakura Walking Tour 5.0 (146), GetYourGuide Kamakura and Enoshima Day Trip 4.6 (1,748) [S44, S45]. Single-author blog (walked 2026-09-13) documents the trail [S37].
Location and access
JR from Tokyo or Shinagawa to Kita-Kamakura (about 55-65 minutes) and Enoden for Hase (iteration 1) [S49]. The trail start is 11 minutes from Kita-Kamakura Station [S37]. The exit is 6 minutes from Kotoku-in and 11 minutes from Hase Station [S37].
Time and physical effort
The trail is 3.59 km, 85 minutes including the detour or about 54 minutes without it, with about 198 m of climbing and a high point near 100 m [S37]. AllTrails lists a 2.2 mi (3.5 km) point-to-point route with 308 ft (94 m) of ascent in 1-1.5 hours [S39]. Add the two temples for 4-6 hours in all.
Approximate cost
The trail is free. Kotoku-in is 300 yen (interior 50), 08:00-17:00 from October to March; Hase-dera is 400 yen, 08:00-17:00 with last entry 16:30 [S08, S09]. Transport about 2,200-2,700 JR and Enoden return, or the Odakyu Enoshima-Kamakura pass at 1,640 (iteration 1) [S15, S49].
Crowds and booking
No booking. The trail itself is not crowded in the blog's account; the Great Buddha and Hase-dera are busy at weekends (model knowledge, unverified).
Late-November / early-December fit
A fallen-tree closure on the trail was lifted in March 2026 [S37]; no other 2026 closure was found. Foliage is a late-November to early-December window on the Ten-en side [S28]. Temples close by 17:00 and dusk is about 16:28 [S08, S09, S14], so finish by about 15:30.
Safety and constraints
Earth, roots, mud and rock slabs; one vending machine at Kuzuharaoka Shrine, then no water; one toilet block in Genjiyama Park [S37]. Wear grippy shoes. Street snacks on Komachi-dori were not screened for pork.
Uncertainty / what to verify
The trail condition after rain; the Hase-dera and Kotoku-in hours on the day; Reddit not opened.

Cost per adultFree
Time2.5-4 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaChiyoda and Chuo, Tokyo

Assumptions. Prices use an assumed 150 JPY per USD; iteration 1 figures marked as such were not rechecked; central Tokyo transit is an allowance of 400-800 yen per outing. Distances marked "my estimate" are not measured.

Bear notices. The Takao Trail 1 sighting (Aug 20, 70 m off the trail, alert level 1) is confirmed by two news reports [S18, S19]; the regional counts are from tracking sites that include incidents outside the mountains [S20, S21, S22]; the Okutama fatality report is from May 2026 [S23]. Hakone and Kyoto statuses are snippet-only [S47]. Follow the same minimum everywhere: daylight, a group, a bell or radio, and a current notice check.

Booking windows. Ordinary walks need no reservation. The Rikugien night illumination has online advance tickets at 1,000 (1,200 same day) and I did not find the sales-start date (item 08) [S10]. The highway bus and the Romancecar need reservations (items 09, 10). The Imperial Household Agency inner-grounds tour needs a booking (item 07).

Closures on the trip dates. Fri 27 Nov: Imperial East Gardens closed. Sun 29 Nov: the last day of the Kawaguchiko Momiji Festival. Mon 30 Nov: Shinjuku Gyoen and the Imperial East Gardens are closed, the Takao Visitor Center is closed, and the Inokashira zoo is closed [S02, S04, S05, S30]. Mt Takao Trail 6 is uphill-only all day from Oct 31 to Dec 6 [S01]. The Rikugien night event runs Nov 25 to Dec 6 [S10]. Wed 25 Nov is arrival evening only and Wed 2 Dec is morning only.

Weather and daylight. Tokyo sunset is about 16:28-16:29 [S14]; forest shade arrives earlier, so finish wooded routes by about 15:30. Japan Guide's colour windows: Tokyo late November to early December, Kyoto mid November to early December, Fuji Five Lakes late October to mid November, Nikko mid October to early November [S35]. Leaves, moss and frost make stone and wood steps slippery. No forecast is available this far out.

Safety and legal. Crowds are the main concern at Takeshita Street, Senso-ji, Fushimi Inari and Higashiyama; keep valuables in front. Pork and alcohol: shrine, garden and trail visits have no pork or alcohol requirement, but street snacks (Ameyoko, Yanaka Ginza, Nakamise, Komachi-dori), the Azabudai Hills market, Harmonica Yokocho and any tour lunch should be checked; no package or stall is guaranteed pork-free. Decline tout invitations in nightlife districts such as Roppongi.
