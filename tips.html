2. Four Simple Rules to Reset Our Clock in 3 Days

☀️ 1. No Sunglasses for First 20 Mins Outside (Thu–Sat)

  • Bright morning outdoor daylight between 07:00–08:30 tells your brain's master clock the sun just rose, shifting you onto Tokyo time twice as fast.

⏱️ 2. Cap Afternoon Power Naps at 30–45 Mins

  • Always set an alarm during your Thu/Sat hotel naps and follow with an iced matcha or coffee so you don't wake up wide awake at midnight.

💊 3. Take 0.5mg–1mg Melatonin at 21:00 (Nights 1–3)

  • 11:00 PM Tokyo = 8:00 AM Chicago. Low-dose melatonin at 21:00 prevents your Chicago body clock from giving you a midnight second wind.

✈️ 4. Plane Sleep Strategy on Wednesday

  • Sleep during the first half of the flight and stay awake for the last 4–5 hours before landing at 17:45 so you sleep deeply on Night 1.

4. Communication, Menus & Language Barrier Hacks

Tokyo and Hakone are effortless to navigate with zero Japanese—every train sign, ticket machine, and convenience store ATM has an English button, and most restaurants use picture tablets or touchscreen kiosks. Here is how to handle everything else seamlessly:

📷 1. Google Translate Camera Lens (Menus & 7-Eleven Labels)

  • Live Menu & Machine Translation: Open Google Translate → Camera and point it at vertical paper menus, ramen vending machines, or bathhouse signs—it overlays English directly on the image in real time.
  • Instant Ingredient Check: Point the Camera Lens at the back of any 7-Eleven sandwich, snack, or sauce bottle to instantly spot pork (豚肉), gelatin (ゼラチン), lard (ラード), or alcohol (アルコール / お酒).

📱 2. Show the Screen + Speak Short Phrases

  • Show, Don't Ramble: Japanese staff read written English/Japanese much better than they catch fast spoken English sentences. Use the Conversation tab in Google Translate or Apple Translate and turn your screen toward them.
  • 1-Tap Chef Card: Keep a screenshot of your No-Pork / No-Alcohol Chef Card (from the Checklist page) in your iPhone Favorites album and show it right when you sit down or order.

🗣️ 3. The Only 5 Phrases + 1 Hand Gesture You Need

  • Sumimasen (Soo-mee-mah-sen) — "Excuse me" (getting a waiter's attention or passing in a crowd).
  • Kore onegaishimasu (Koh-reh oh-neh-gai-shee-mas) — "This one, please" (while pointing at a menu photo or display case).
  • Mizu onegaishimasu (Mee-zoo oh-neh-gai-shee-mas) — "Water, please."
  • Okaikei onegaishimasu (Oh-kai-kay...) — "Check/bill, please" — or simply cross your two index fingers in an "X" (Japan's universal hand sign for the check).
  • Arigato gozaimasu (Ah-ree-gah-toh go-zai-mas) — "Thank you very much."

📶 4. Offline Prep (Subways & Mountain Tunnels)

  • Download Offline Japanese Before Flying: In both Google Translate and iPhone's built-in Translate app, download the Japanese language pack on home Wi-Fi so camera and voice translation work instantly underground or in Hakone/Fuji tunnels.
  • Download Offline Google Maps: Save offline maps of Tokyo and Hakone / Lake Kawaguchiko in Google Maps so walking pins never lag.
