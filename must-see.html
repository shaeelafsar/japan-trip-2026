Quick take

  • Four A-tier picks, all in Tokyo: Meiji Jingu, Senso-ji, Shinjuku Gyoen and the Rikugien night light-up (Nov 25 - Dec 6). Shibuya Crossing moved down to B: it is a short free spectacle.
  • Kyoto, Nara and Osaka are a swap, not an add-on. They cost about ¥28,000-31,000 per adult by bullet train return and need an overnight stay, so they replace Tokyo days.
  • Daylight is short. Sunset is about 16:30, Meiji Jingu closes at 16:10 (16:00 in December), and several places close Monday, Nov 30 (Shinjuku Gyoen, Tokyo National Museum, Imperial East Gardens).
  • Fuji is weather-dependent. Summit climbing, Kamikochi and the Tateyama Kurobe route are closed or out of season, and Himeji Castle was left out (scaffolding until July 2027).
  • Redone with Google Maps, TripAdvisor and official pages. Reddit stayed blocked, so no thread was read and no pick is rated high confidence.

1

Meiji Jingu (with Harajuku if wanted)

A · Strongest fit

A quiet forest shrine in the middle of the city, then the fashion streets of Harajuku if you want them. Free, easy and the strongest all-round fit. The gate closes at 16:10 in November, so go in the morning or early afternoon.

What to see: The forest approach and main shrine are the core. The Inner Garden (about 500) and the museum (about 1,000) are optional paid extras per a blog [S33]. Harajuku and Omotesando are an add-on for another 1.5-2.5 hours (iteration 1) [S73].

Cost per adultFree
Time1-1.5 hours; 2.5-4 with Harajuku
ConfidenceMedium
AreaShibuya, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Gate closes at 16:10 in November, so it is a morning or early-afternoon stop.

Why it ranks here: Free, central and the best-rated big Tokyo sight on Google Maps (4.7 (53,883)); the main constraint is that the gate closes before 16:30.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps 4.7 (53,883) [S60]; TripAdvisor 4.4 (8,517) [S63]; marketplace guided walks Viator 5.0 (591) and 5.0 (539), GetYourGuide 4.9 (620) and 4.9 (433) [S66, S68] (Viator and TripAdvisor tour counts are one pool). Blogs: Beautifully Travelled (Sep 2026) says it "costs nothing, takes about an hour" [S33]; Japan Local Experience (updated 2026-10-05) says to go early [S35]. Both are single-author posts, not counts.
Location and access
Harajuku or Meiji-jingumae stations (iteration 1) [S73]. Allow 400-800 yen return.
Time and physical effort
About 1 hour at the shrine [S33]; 2.5-4 hours with Harajuku (iteration 1) [S73]. Wide gravel paths and a lot of standing.
Approximate cost
Free. Inner Garden 500 and museum 1,000 are blog figures, not read on an official page [S33].
Crowds and booking
No booking. The shrine opens at sunrise, and the earlier you go the calmer it is [S03, S35]. I did not find a queue-time source. Mid-November children's festival crowds (Shichi-go-san) may still linger (model knowledge, unverified).
Late-November / early-December fit
The official hours are November 06:10-16:10 and December 06:40-16:00; some facilities inside close earlier (mainly 09:00-16:00) [S03]. Wed 2 Dec is usable only in the morning, and the shrine is not an arrival-evening option.
Safety and constraints
Active worship site: follow photography signs. Gravel is awkward for wheeled luggage. No pork or alcohol requirement.
Uncertainty / what to verify
Inner Garden and museum prices and hours (blog only); any weekend wedding or festival crowds; Reddit not opened.

Senso-ji and Asakusa

A · Strongest fit
Sonnet Memory Overlap

Tokyo's oldest and busiest temple. Walk under the giant red Kaminarimon gate and up Nakamise street, a lane of snack and souvenir stalls, to the main hall. Go early, because it is crowded after about 09:00.

What to see: Kaminarimon gate, Nakamise approach, main hall and pagoda; side streets west of the temple add local colour (iteration 1) [S73].

Cost per adultFree
Time2-3 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaTaito, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Crowded after about 09:00; the temple is best very early and the shops open later.

Why it ranks here: The most-reviewed Tokyo temple and the only major sight whose grounds are open around the clock; go at about 06:00-07:00 for the temple and come back later for the shops.

Memory overlap: Kimono rental and stroll in Asakusa (same area; now researched in iteration 2). The rank was reviewed against it and left as is.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps Senso-ji 4.6 (100,309), Kaminarimon 4.5 (38,766), Nakamise 4.3 (15,870) [S60]; TripAdvisor Senso-ji 4.4 (9,895) and Asakusa 4.4 (6,220) [S63]; marketplace Asakusa walking tours Viator 5.0 (1,228), 4.9 (1,125) and 5.0 (1,015), GetYourGuide 4.8 (1,993) and 4.9 (991), Klook about 4.9 (304) [S66, S68, S67] (Viator and TripAdvisor tour counts are one pool). The Japansophy post (published Jun 25, updated Sep 27) says it is "absolutely still worth a visit" and best at 6:00 am [S31]. The shopping street rates lowest of the three Google listings, so the temple is the draw, not Nakamise (my inference).
Location and access
Asakusa station (Ginza and Asakusa lines; operators' exits differ) (iteration 1) [S73]. Allow 400-800 yen return.
Time and physical effort
2-3 hours, mostly level with some steps (iteration 1) [S73].
Approximate cost
Free. Allow street-snack money; prices were not recorded.
Crowds and booking
No booking. The grounds are open 24 hours; the main hall opens at about 06:00-06:30 (the same blog gives both) and closes about 17:00 in October to March; Nakamise shops run about 09:00-18:00 [S31]. Weekend 28-29 Nov will be the busiest (model knowledge, unverified).
Late-November / early-December fit
No seasonal closure. The official site lists the Hagoita Market on 17-19 December, which is after the trip [S04]. An arrival-evening visit is physically possible because the grounds stay open, but I did not verify evening lighting (model knowledge, unverified).
Safety and constraints
Active worship site; incense areas can be smoky. Some Nakamise snacks, such as menchi-katsu, contain pork (model knowledge, unverified); ask before buying.
Uncertainty / what to verify
Hall opening time (06:00 or 06:30); shop hours vary; the Girl on a Zebra post I tried landed on a blog index page and is not used [S31].

Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden

A · Strongest fit
Sonnet Memory Overlap

A large landscaped garden with Japanese, English and French sections and a greenhouse. Late-November colour is likely but not guaranteed, and it is a calm break from the city. Closed Monday, Nov 30.

What to see: Pick one or two sections (the Japanese garden, the lawns) rather than covering everything; leave time to sit.

Cost per adult¥500
Time1.5-3 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaShinjuku, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Closed Monday 30 Nov; last entry 16:00; foliage is a window, not a promise.

Why it ranks here: A large, well-rated city garden at the right foliage window; choose it on any day except Monday 30 Nov.

Memory overlap: Hamarikyu Gardens (another garden). The rank was reviewed against it and left as is.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps 4.6 (46,023) [S60]; TripAdvisor 4.6 (6,391) [S63]. The GO TOKYO foliage guide (updated 2026-09-01) puts Shinjuku Gyoen's colour at mid-November to early December [S23]. Roam Japan's autumn comparison picks Shinjuku Gyoen as its first choice for a short city outing and says to enter before the 16:00 last entry [S27].
Location and access
The Shinjuku Gate is about 5 minutes from Exit 1 of Shinjuku-gyoemmae Station [S27]; Sendagaya and Shinjuku-side gates also exist (iteration 1) [S73]. Allow 400-800 yen return.
Time and physical effort
1.5-3 hours; gravel, slopes and steps [S27].
Approximate cost
Adult 500; 65 and over and students 250 [S01].
Crowds and booking
No booking needed. I did not find a queue-time source. Colour-peak days attract clusters of visitors (model knowledge, unverified).
Late-November / early-December fit
October 1 to March 14 hours are 09:00-16:30, last entry 16:00. Closed Mondays, so Mon 30 Nov is closed [S01].
Safety and constraints
A managed national garden; follow the rules and the gate deadline. Alcohol and tripods are restricted (model knowledge, unverified).
Uncertainty / what to verify
The foliage peak for the specific day; the official English page was not read (the Japanese page was).

Rikugien night illumination

A · Strongest fit
Sonnet Memory Overlap

A classic Edo-period stroll garden lit up at night, with maples reflected in the pond. The light-up runs Nov 25 to Dec 6, 18:00-20:30, on a separate ticket. It is the best evening plan after you land.

What to see: The night illumination (separate evening admission after the daytime closing) with this year's new projection mapping [S05]. Daytime admission is a separate, cheaper ticket.

Cost per adult¥1,000 advance; ¥1,200 same day
Time1-1.5 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaBunkyo, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Advance sales dates were not announced and daily caps apply; cold evening.

Why it ranks here: The only A item with a dated, priced evening event that falls inside the trip (official, 2026), and a cheap one; daytime Rikugien is a weaker option at the same garden.

Memory overlap: Hamarikyu Gardens (another garden). The rank was reviewed against it and left as is.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps 4.4 (10,745) [S60]. The official page gives the 2026 illumination dates and prices [S05]. GO TOKYO puts Rikugien's colour at late November to mid-December [S23]. Two blogs dated Aug 2026 (Designer Journeys, Aug 6; Olachill, Aug 12) write up the autumn light-up, but they are single-author accounts and do not add a count [S36, S38]. No traveler count was recorded on TripAdvisor.
Location and access
Komagome Station area, Bunkyo (iteration 1) [S73]. Allow 400-800 yen return.
Time and physical effort
1-1.5 hours on a flat strolling garden; cold after dark.
Approximate cost
Evening admission 1,000 advance (online only) or 1,200 same day. Regular daytime entry is 300, 09:00-17:00 [S05].
Crowds and booking
Online sales dates were not announced and daily caps apply [S05]. Buy as soon as the sale opens; arrive before 19:30 last entry.
Late-November / early-December fit
Illumination runs Wed 25 Nov to Sun 6 Dec, 18:00-20:30, last entry 19:30 [S05], so every full day is covered. The arrival evening (Wed 25 Nov) is not realistic because the flight lands at 17:45.
Safety and constraints
Uneven paths in the dark; dress warmly. No pork or alcohol requirement. Pre-sold tickets are online-only, so a data connection is needed.
Uncertainty / what to verify
When advance sales open; whether same-day tickets remain on busy weekends (28-29 Nov); the daily cap; the exact projection-mapping content.
Sources
S05, S23, S36, S38, S60, S73

Tokyo Metropolitan Government observatory and Shinjuku neon

B · Worth it if it fits

A free observation deck high above Shinjuku with views to the Tokyo skyline and, on a clear day, Mount Fuji. Pair it with a walk through the neon streets below. The south deck is closed Tuesday, Dec 1.

What to see: Pick the south deck for the longer evening hours; walk through the Shinjuku streets after dark for the neon.

Cost per adultFree
Time1.5-3 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaShinjuku, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: 20-30 minute queue; the south deck is closed Tuesday 1 Dec.

Why it ranks here: A free 202-metre skyline view that is a strong substitute for paid decks; it earns B, not A, because of the queue and the weather dependence.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps north deck 4.5 (9,394) and south deck 4.7 (3,271) [S60]; TripAdvisor 4.4 (5,617) for the two decks [S63]. The official page says Mt Fuji is visible to the west and that days with clear air are most frequent from December to February [S07].
Location and access
The first main building, 45th floor, reached by a dedicated lift from the ground floor; Shinjuku Station or Tochomae Station (iteration 1) [S07, S73]. Allow 400-800 yen return.
Time and physical effort
1.5-3 hours with queue and neon walk; mostly level.
Approximate cost
Free. No advance or group reservations are accepted [S07].
Crowds and booking
Expect a 20-30 minute queue; it varies by weather and hour [S07]. Wait times are shown as available (0-5 minutes), somewhat busy (5-30) and busy (30+) [S07].
Late-November / early-December fit
The south deck is open 09:30-22:00 (last entry 21:30) and closed on the 1st and 3rd Tuesday, so Tue 1 Dec is closed; on that day the north deck stays open to 22:00. The north deck is normally 09:30-17:30 [S07]. Fuji visibility is more common in December than in late November (inference from [S07]).
Safety and constraints
Tripods are not allowed [S07]. The decks may close at short notice in bad weather [S07].
Uncertainty / what to verify
The south and north deck schedule PDF for Dec 1; Fuji visibility on the chosen day.

Hamarikyu Gardens

B · Worth it if it fits

A tidal-pond garden with tea houses and a skyline of Ginza and Shiodome towers behind it. Cheap, central and flat. Closes at 17:00 and has a smaller evidence base than the bigger gardens.

What to see: The tidal pond and its tea houses, with the Ginza and Shiodome towers behind; one blogger saw construction on one of the northern bridges in Nov 2025 but had other paths [S41].

Cost per adult¥300
Time1-1.5 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaChuo, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Closes at 17:00 and the evidence base is smaller than the other gardens.

Why it ranks here: A tidal-pond garden against the skyline, cheap and central, promoted from a lead to B on a solid official page and a late-November colour window.

1-1.5 hours; flat gravel paths.

Tokyo National Museum and Ueno Park

B · Worth it if it fits

Japan's biggest museum for art and artifacts (samurai swords, kimono, ancient pottery), next to Ueno Park. The Toyokan closes Nov 30 and the museum is closed Monday, Nov 30. Adult entry is ¥1,000.

What to see: Pick the Honkan (Japanese Gallery) and one or two other buildings rather than all of them; Ueno Park is the free outdoor complement.

Cost per adult¥1,000
Time2.5-4 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaUeno, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: Interest-dependent; closed Monday 30 Nov and the Toyokan closes Nov 30.

Why it ranks here: Japan's largest museum and a good bad-weather anchor; the late-November closures shrink its December scope.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps Tokyo National Museum 4.5 (31,654) and Ueno Park 4.4 (35,050) [S60]; TripAdvisor Tokyo National Museum 4.3 (2,616) and Ueno Park 4.3 (5,417) [S63]. I did not open a usable blog for this item (the one I tried landed on a homepage) [S73].
Location and access
About 10 minutes' walk from JR Ueno or Uguisudani Station and 15 minutes from Ueno on the Ginza and Hibiya lines [S02]. Allow 400-800 yen return.
Time and physical effort
2.5-4 hours; a lot of standing, but indoors.
Approximate cost
Adults 1,000 and university students 500 for the collection exhibitions [S02]. Iteration 1 recorded 500, which was the student price. Park free.
Crowds and booking
Ticket queues at the main gate can exceed 30 minutes; online tickets are recommended [S02]. Security screening and baggage checks apply; suitcases are not allowed [S02].
Late-November / early-December fit
09:30-17:00, and to 20:00 on Fridays and Saturdays (Fri 27 and Sat 28 Nov); last entry 30 minutes before closing. Closed Mondays, so Mon 30 Nov is closed. The Honkan Rooms 17-20 and the Toyokan close Nov 30 to Dec 31, so those galleries are open only on Nov 25-29. Honkan Room 4 is closed to Apr 25, 2027, and Rooms 2-3 from Dec 14 [S02].
Safety and constraints
Bag screening and no suitcases [S02]. No pork or alcohol requirement.
Uncertainty / what to verify
Which galleries are open on the chosen day, and the special exhibition (listed as "Hiroshige: New Perspectives on Edo Japan") dates and ticket price [S02].

Shibuya Crossing

B · Worth it if it fits

The famous scramble where crowds cross in every direction at once. A short, free spectacle that is over in 30 to 60 minutes. Watch from street level or a nearby window, and find the Hachiko statue.

What to see: Cross it once at street level and watch from a window or the station concourse; combine it with the paid or free viewing options in the Activities report instead of building a trip around it.

Cost per adultFree
Time30-60 minutes
ConfidenceMedium
AreaShibuya, Tokyo

Main tradeoff: A short free spectacle, described by one blogger as basically a big crosswalk.

Why it ranks here: A free, iconic spectacle that earns its rating through volume, but it is a 30-60 minute stop, not a destination; moved down from A in iteration 1.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps 4.5 (24,595) [S60]; TripAdvisor 4.2 (7,931), the lowest TripAdvisor rating among the Tokyo A and B sights [S63]. Travel Lemming (updated Apr 2024) calls the crossing "basically just a big crosswalk" in a post on attractions it found overrated [S32]. Iteration 1 found travelers enjoying the evening crossing and shopping [S73].
Location and access
Shibuya Station; allow extra navigation time in the station complex (iteration 1) [S73]. Allow 400-800 yen return.
Time and physical effort
30-60 minutes; crowded pavements and station stairs (iteration 1) [S73].
Approximate cost
Free.
Crowds and booking
No booking. Early darkness suits it: sunset is around 16:30 in late November (planning figure, model knowledge, unverified), so the lights and screens are on from the late afternoon.
Late-November / early-December fit
No seasonal issue; shops provide partial rain shelter (iteration 1) [S73].
Safety and constraints
Keep moving in the pedestrian flow and decline tout invitations; the UK FCDO flags Shibuya nightlife risks (iteration 1) [S73]. Stay with street sightseeing, not bars. No pork or alcohol requirement.

Kamakura: Great Buddha and Hase-dera

B · Worth it if it fits

The outdoor bronze Great Buddha plus Hase-dera, a hillside temple with sea views. An easy day trip from Tokyo (about an hour each way). Temples close early, so start in the morning.

What to see: The Great Buddha at Kotoku-in, then about 5 minutes downhill to Hase-dera for the Kannon statue and the ocean-view terrace [S26]. Tsurugaoka Hachimangu is the optional third stop.

Cost per adult¥700 entries
Time4-6 hours
ConfidenceMedium
AreaKanagawa

Main tradeoff: Full day with about 1 hour each way; temple closing times are early.

Why it ranks here: The best-documented day trip from Tokyo (about 1 hour by train) with two strongly rated temples; keep it to the Great Buddha, Hase-dera and one more stop.

More details and sources
Traveler evidence
Seen 2026-10-19/20: Google Maps Hase-dera 4.5 (17,371), Kotoku-in 4.4 (33,168), Tsurugaoka Hachimangu 4.4 (29,577) [S61]; TripAdvisor Hasedera 4.5 (1,791), Kotoku-in 4.3 (3,154), Tsurugaoka 4.2 (1,653) [S63]; marketplace Kamakura and Enoshima day tours Klook about 4.8 (1.5K, rounded) and 4.7 (457), Viator 4.5 (274) [S69] (Viator and TripAdvisor tour counts are one pool). Tokyo Cheapo's Kamakura guide and The Invisible Tourist's day-trip post lay out the Great Buddha to Hase route as a one-day plan [S26, S43].
Location and access
Tokyo-Shinagawa-Yokosuka Line to Kamakura, then the Enoden to Hase (iteration 1) [S73]; about 1 hour from Tokyo [S26]. Allow roughly 990-1,040 yen each way [S26, S73] plus 400-800 locally.
Time and physical effort
4-6 hours locally (iteration 1) [S73]. Hase-dera has steps; the Great Buddha precinct is easier.
Approximate cost
Kotoku-in 300 and Hase-dera 400 = 700 (iteration 1) [S13, S73]; the Buddha interior is 50 [S13]. A Google result snippet quoted 20 for the interior, which the official page contradicts [S70].
Crowds and booking
No booking. Kamakura is busy on weekends (model knowledge, unverified), so a weekday is better than Sat 28 or Sun 29 Nov.
Late-November / early-December fit
Kotoku-in is open 08:00-17:00 from October to March, with the interior to 16:30 [S13]; Tsurugaoka's gates stay open 06:00-20:00 [S14]. I did not verify the Hase-dera closing time (model knowledge, about 17:00 in winter, unverified). Sunset is around 16:30, so start early.
Safety and constraints
Steps and slopes at Hase-dera. Whitebait rice bowls are common lunches (fish, no pork), but check the broth and toppings [S26].
Uncertainty / what to verify

Weather and season. Safety and legal. Crowds are the main concern at Senso-ji, Shibuya, Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera and Arashiyama; keep valuables in front and move with the flow. Chureito has 398 steps (item 10) and the Rikugien illumination paths are uneven in the dark (item 04). Pork and alcohol: shrine and garden visits have no pork or alcohol requirement, but street snacks in Asakusa, Kamakura and Osaka, Gion restaurants, the Yokohama Christmas Market (sausages and mulled wine) and any tour lunch should be checked; no package or stall is guaranteed pork-free.
